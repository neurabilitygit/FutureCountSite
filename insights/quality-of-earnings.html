Quality of earnings: what fintech CEOs need to prove before a sale

Public disclosures from Block, Coinbase, Upstart and Green Dot show why revenue, adjusted profit and available cash need to be understood together.

Eric Bass·6 minute read

In this article

Why it mattersBlockCoinbaseUpstartGreen DotWhat to prepare

A buyer can accept your financial statements and still disagree about what the business earns.

That difference matters when you are preparing to sell. The transaction depends on a view of future performance: which revenues will continue, what it costs to deliver them, how much cash the business needs and what changes when ownership changes.

A quality of earnings review establishes that view by testing how sustainable the earnings are and how reliably they convert to cash. Where an audit checks whether historical statements follow accounting rules, a QoE review tests the economics a buyer is actually paying for.

Doing that work before going to market lets you investigate a concern, fix a weakness or document a legitimate adjustment before a buyer’s diligence team turns it into a price reduction.

The financial impact can be substantial. In an illustrative transaction priced at eight times EBITDA, a $1 million reduction in accepted annual earnings reduces enterprise value by $8 million, assuming the multiple stays unchanged. The appropriate valuation measure and multiple depend on the business.

Fintech adds another layer. Revenue may include amounts that largely pass through the business. Earnings may depend on interest rates, lending capacity or partner agreements. Cash on the balance sheet may carry obligations that limit its availability.

Four public disclosures illustrate the kinds of questions that come up in diligence.

Gross volume vs. economic margin: Block

In 2023, Cash App reported $9.50 billion of bitcoin revenue and $205 million of bitcoin gross profit. That is approximately 2.2% of the reported revenue. Block explained that bitcoin revenue represents the total amount sold to customers, while the corresponding costs represent the amount paid to acquire it. Block’s Q4 2023 shareholder letter, page 18

Two businesses with similar top-line revenue can have completely different margins and cash needs. Before a sale, connect customer activity to gross profit by product and show the net contribution after servicing, processing, fraud and credit costs.

Recurring revenue vs. rate-sensitive revenue: Coinbase

Its stablecoin revenue increased from $245.7 million in 2022 to $694.2 million in 2023, a 183% increase. The company attributed higher stablecoin revenue to higher average interest rates earned on USDC reserves, which rose 325 basis points. This revenue sat within its subscription and services category. Coinbase’s 2023 Form 10-K, pages 97–98

Putting a revenue stream inside a “subscription and services” line does not make it behave like a multi-year software contract. Revenue can repeat every month while still depending on interest rates, customer balances or partner revenue-share terms.

Before going to a buyer, break revenue growth down into customer count, usage, pricing, balances and rate sensitivity, and show what happens to earnings when those drivers move.

Adjusted EBITDA vs. cash consumption: Upstart

In 2022, it reported $37.2 million of adjusted EBITDA and a $108.7 million GAAP net loss. Its EBITDA reconciliation excluded $128.0 million of stock compensation and certain payroll taxes. Upstart also described stock compensation as a significant recurring expense. Upstart’s full-year 2022 results

The same results showed $674.7 million of operating cash outflow, including substantial purchases and sales of loans held for sale. That outflow requires analysis of lending activity and funding; it cannot simply be treated as overhead losses.

A buyer will look at earnings adjustments and working-capital or funding demands together. Any expense excluded from adjusted EBITDA still needs an economic explanation, and growth that consumes balance-sheet cash needs a clear funding plan.

One-time penalties vs. ongoing compliance cost: Green Dot

In July 2024, the Federal Reserve imposed a $44 million penalty for consumer compliance failures. It also required improvements to compliance and anti-money-laundering programs, including independent third-party work. Federal Reserve announcement, July 19, 2024

A buyer separates a historical fine from what it costs to run the business compliantly going forward. Even when a past penalty is a valid one-time add-back, the ongoing cost of people, systems, monitoring and controls stays in run-rate EBITDA.

If you have open regulatory findings or remediation work, build a costed plan with compliance leadership and counsel before diligence starts, showing what is closed, what remains open and what the permanent cost base looks like.

What to prepare.

Taken together, these examples point to a preparation standard that a CEO can put into practice. I would ask the team to produce five connected pieces of work:

  • A reconciled monthly financial record. Financial statements and operating metrics should trace to the accounting system, with explanations for differences between management reporting and statutory accounts. Contracts, invoices and cash records should support material revenue.
  • An evidence-backed adjustment schedule. Show the amount, period, supporting document and expected future treatment of every adjustment. Include costs needed to sustain the business, such as replacement leadership and previously underfunded functions.
  • A revenue and dependency analysis. Show customer retention, concentration, margins and acquisition economics. Identify reliance on key banks, processors and funding partners, and have counsel assess relevant contract provisions.
  • A cash-and-capital model. Distinguish operating cash generation from investment and funding needs. Separate company cash from customer funds and restricted balances, and assess settlement and regulatory capital requirements with the appropriate specialists.
  • An action plan with named owners. Each material finding should have an accountable person, supporting evidence and a target date. Bring the independent QoE provider, banker and counsel into the process at the appropriate stage.

The carve-out consideration

For a carve-out, add a clear view of standalone economics. Shared finance, technology, leadership and compliance services may need to be replaced. Separate those continuing costs from one-time separation spending and temporary transition services. Historical allocations need to be tested against the actual cost of operating the business after separation.

This work should begin early enough to influence readiness. A well-organized data room is useful, but it becomes persuasive when the underlying numbers reconcile, the assumptions are explicit and the unresolved issues have a credible plan.
