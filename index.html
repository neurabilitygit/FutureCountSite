Ready for the
next owner.

I help owners and CEOs strengthen and prepare companies for an ownership transition or strategic sale, and stay involved until the work is done.

I’m Eric Bass, a CFO and adviser with more than 30 years in financial services. Owners make their biggest decisions (when to invest, who will lead, whether and how to sell) without a clear view of the whole business. I created FutureCount to give them that view, and the hands-on help to act on it, from execution to outcome.

Where I’m most useful.

I work best with owner-led companies where you still make the calls, whether a transition is a few years out or already underway. Usually the books are kept for taxes rather than a buyer, the business has outgrown its reporting, or a buyer is already asking questions the team can’t answer cleanly.

Preparing
for a sale.

A buyer will test every number you present. The companies that hold their value are prepared before the buyer arrives.

  • Get the numbers ready

    Clean up and, where needed, separate the financial statements; support adjusted EBITDA; find the issues a quality of earnings review would find.

  • Build the data room

    Organize the documents a buyer will ask for, before they ask.

  • Run diligence from the company’s side

    Act as the buyer’s day-to-day contact and keep requests moving, so you can keep running the business.

  • Plan the transition

    Show a buyer who will lead the company after the sale.

Decisions business owners
are weighing today.

These choices influence one another. Making them well takes a clear view of the whole business and the future you want for it.

  • Growth and investment

    A promising growth opportunity can also require more cash, people and management attention. The decision rests on how those demands fit together.

  • People and leadership

    Who runs the company next, how you develop and keep key people, and what that means for your own role.

  • Technology and AI

    What to invest in, and what will actually pay back. The useful starting point is the work you want to improve.

  • Ownership and personal wealth

    Your timing, executive incentives, family goals, tax position and a buyer’s terms all shape what a transition means for you. Continued family ownership may also be part of the plan.
