Financial services operators.

Broker-dealers, fintechs and the firms that serve them carry obligations a generalist CFO has never had to answer for. Reporting deadlines run on their own calendar. Revenue rests on agreements with banks, custodians and clearing firms. And a change of ownership is itself a regulated event.

A regulated business is not
just a business.

In a regulated company the financial statements are the beginning of the answer, not the end of it.

Most advisory work assumes a clean profit and loss statement settles the question. Here it does not. A buyer has to know what obligations survive the closing, which filings are still outstanding, and what your exam history says about the business they are inheriting.

I have spent my career on that side of the table: as a consulting partner to banks and capital markets firms, as a practice leader, and now as a CFO. I know what a counterparty and a buyer each look for, and where those two views disagree.

On a regulated transaction I work alongside your regulatory reporting team, regulatory counsel and FinOps to identify what the deal depends on and plan an orderly exit. I coordinate that work; I don’t replace the specialists in the room.

Where I work.

Four kinds of engagement, usually overlapping.

  • Regulatory dependencies

    What the transaction touches — reporting obligations, licences, member and counterparty consents, and the finance operations behind them — and the sequence for an orderly exit.

  • Transaction support

    Readiness, the data room, earnings quality and the company-side work through closing, including the approvals a regulated buyer has to obtain before the deal can complete.

  • Carve-outs and stand-alone financials

    Separating a division or a business line from its parent: cost allocation, shared services, and financial statements that stand up on their own.

  • Fintech economics

    Unit economics, revenue quality, bank and partner agreements, funding preparation and the board reporting that goes with a regulated balance sheet.

What a buyer tests
in a regulated business.

The financial review is the easy part. These are the questions that move the timetable.

  • What the transaction touches

    Reporting obligations, licences, member agreements and the finance operations that support them — and whether anything lapses the morning after closing.

  • Revenue that survives the transfer

    Agreements with banks, custodians, clearing firms and introducers: assignability, termination rights, notice periods and concentration.

  • The supervisory record

    Examination findings, remediation and anything a buyer’s counsel will want to see long before the letter of intent.

  • Stand-alone cost

    What the business actually costs to run once the parent’s shared services, systems and people are priced honestly.

  • The people who hold it together

    Who owns the licences, the relationships and the controls, and what the buyer is really acquiring when they leave.

Before we begin.

Do you work only with financial services firms?

No. The rest of this site describes work with owner-led companies in other sectors. This page exists because regulation changes the answer, and the same language should not be asked to cover both.

Will you work alongside our compliance team and counsel?

Yes. On regulated matters I work with your compliance officer, outside counsel and auditors. I do not replace any of them, and I do not give legal advice.

What is different about selling a broker-dealer?

A change of ownership at a member firm is itself a filing, and the buyer has to satisfy the regulator that the business will be properly supervised and financed from day one. That shapes the timetable, the data room and what has to be true before signing rather than after.

Do you take interim CFO roles?

Occasionally, when a transaction or a transition needs someone in the seat for a period. My current CFO work runs through FutureCount.

How do we start?

A 30-minute conversation. If it is a fit, I will send a short proposal with the scope, deliverables and timing.
